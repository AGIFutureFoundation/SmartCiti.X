# First responders

A training scaffold for the emergency services and disaster relief:
**5 services**, **30 role tiers**,
**62 competency domains**, **25 scenario
frames** and **152 cross-links** that tie a frame to
**81 of the 111** trade halls this bundle
already builds. It reaches a learner through the 🚒 panel in the
[3D app](../web/trade_craft_3d.html), and from the other end: a hall a frame
names carries a badge in its own header back into that panel.

## Before anything else

> Nothing in this pack has been reviewed by a serving firefighter, a police officer, a paramedic, an emergency manager or a licensed social worker. Not one of the 117 training items carries a sign-off - the number is 0 of 117 - and it must not be used as the basis of any real certification, qualification, hiring decision or operational procedure.

> AUTHORED: every word of this pack was written here. No standards document was opened, no page was fetched and no model produced any of it. This environment has no network - NFPA, NREMT, FEMA, the state POST commissions and the social work boards are all unreachable from it - so all 23 authority records carry document_read_by_this_build false and none carries a URL. A citation nobody opened is a costume.

> This is a scaffold: role taxonomy, competency domains, scenario frames and debrief structures. It is not a protocol and contains no procedure, no drug, no dose, no triage cut-off, no vital-sign threshold, no tactical technique and no flow, pressure or air figure. 8 patterns for exactly those things are scanned over the finished registry and the build fails on any of them. Where a reader wants the real requirement, every one of the 117 items names the body that owns it.

### A house fire at three in the morning

*a two-storey timber-framed dwelling in a residential street*

Companies arrive to a working fire in an occupied dwelling. The first-arriving officer has a partial account from a neighbour about who lives there and no confirmation about who is out.

**The pressure.** Incomplete information about occupancy, a short window in which the first decisions foreclose the later ones, and a crowd whose expectations are not the officer's risk frame.

**This frame is not** a tactical worksheet. No assignment, order of operations or entry decision is recorded here or implied by the questions.

**Trade halls it touches.** [Carpenters](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=carpenters), [Drywall Finishers](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=drywall), [Roofers & Waterproofers](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=roofers), [Heat & Frost Insulators](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=insulators), [Electrical Workers](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=electricians)

**A debrief would ask:**

1. At what moment did the first officer's picture of occupancy change, and who else knew?
2. What information was the commit decision made on, and how old was it?
3. Who could name the incident commander at any given minute, and who could not?
4. What did the crowd change about the decision, and was that acknowledged out loud?
5. What would have had to be true for the decision to go the other way?

### A commercial building whose suppression system is out of service

*a mid-rise commercial occupancy under partial renovation*

A fire is reported in a building where the sprinkler system has been impaired for contractor work and the alarm panel has been in trouble for two days. The impairment paperwork exists somewhere.

**The pressure.** A protection assumption that the building normally carries has been removed, quietly, by a process that is nobody on the incident's fault.

**This frame is not** a guide to suppression system operation or impairment procedure. Both belong to the code and to the building owner.

**Trade halls it touches.** [Fire Sprinkler Fitters](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=fire-sprinkler), [Fire Alarm Technicians](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=fire-alarm), [Building Automation Techs](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=bas-controls), [Firestop Installers](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=firestop), [Plumbers & Pipefitters](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=pipefitters)

**A debrief would ask:**

1. When did anybody on the incident learn that the system was impaired?
2. What in the pre-incident information would have carried that, and did it?
3. Which assumptions in the initial plan depended on a working system?
4. Who owns the impairment process in this jurisdiction, and is that a finding for them rather than for the crews?
5. What would make this discoverable on arrival next time?

5. What does the facility's own emergency plan say, and had the fire service seen it?

### A fire started by hot work on an occupied site

*a partially occupied building undergoing fit-out*

A fire has started in a concealed space during welding work on an occupied floor of a building under fit-out. The hot work permit system exists. The fire watch had ended.

**The pressure.** A preventable incident where the prevention system existed and did not run, and where the people who could say why are the people who will be blamed.

**This frame is not** a hot work permit procedure or a fire investigation methodology. Both have their own standards and their own qualified people.

**Trade halls it touches.** [Welding Trades](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=welders), [Firestop Installers](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=firestop), [Heat & Frost Insulators](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=insulators), [Site Safety Coordinators](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=site-safety), [Drywall Finishers](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=drywall), [Sheet Metal Workers](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=sheetmetal), [Acoustic Specialists](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=acoustic)

**A debrief would ask:**

1. What did the permit system require, and what actually happened?
2. How did the fire get into the concealed space, and what does that say about the firestopping?
3. How was the investigation separated from the blame, in the first hour?
4. Who on the site knew the fire watch had ended, and who could have stopped the work?
5. Is this a finding for the crews, the contractor or the permit system?

### The days after a line-of-duty death

*an agency, its families and its community*

A responder has been killed on duty. The agency has to continue responding, support a family, cooperate with an investigation, manage a public funeral and hold together a workforce that has just learned the job can do this.

**The pressure.** Every organisational system is loaded at once, the people who would normally manage it are the people affected by it, and every decision is permanent in the family's memory.

**This frame is not** a critical incident stress intervention, a line-of-duty death protocol or a benefits guide. The first is clinical, the second is the agency's and the association's, the third is statutory.

**No trade hall in this bundle is touched by this frame.**

**A debrief would ask:**

1. Who was the family's single point of contact, and how quickly was that named?
2. How was the workforce kept in service without pretending nothing had happened?
3. How were the investigation's needs and the agency's grief kept from colliding?
4. Who supported the supervisors, who were supporting everybody else?
5. What was in place before the day, and what had to be invented?

### An offsite notification from a major energy facility

*a jurisdiction inside a facility's emergency planning zone*

A facility has made a notification that escalates its emergency classification. The jurisdiction's role is protective action for the public, and its information comes entirely from the facility.

**The pressure.** The jurisdiction must make a public protective action decision using technical information it cannot independently verify, from an operator with its own interests, on a timeline set by the facility.

**This frame is not** a radiological emergency plan or a protective action guide. Both are federally governed and neither is stated here.

**Trade halls it touches.** [Nuclear Plant Trades](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=nuclear), [Instrumentation Technicians](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=instrumentation), [Substation Technicians](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=substation), [Transmission Linemen](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=transmission), [Hydro Plant Trades](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=hydro), [District Energy Operators](../web/trade_craft_3d.html?hall=district-energy)

**A debrief would ask:**

1. What did the jurisdiction independently know, and what did it take entirely on trust?
2. How was the protective action decision made and who signed it?
3. How was the message delivered to people who do not speak English and people without power?
4. When was this last exercised, and did the exercise allow failure?
5. What would the jurisdiction have done if the facility's information had been wrong?

## The debrief structure

> The 8-stage debrief structure below is this pack's own scaffold. It is not the federal exercise and evaluation doctrine, and it is not a critical incident stress intervention - the first belongs to FEMA, the second is a clinical activity belonging to licensed people, and confusing either with this would be a real harm.

| # | Stage | The question | Why it is there |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `facts` | **What actually happened, in order** | Establish an agreed sequence before anybody explains anything. Disagreements about the sequence are recorded as disagreements. |
| 2 | `picture` | **What did each person think was going on at the time** | The gap between two people's pictures at the same moment is the finding. This is the question that most often produces one. |
| 3 | `decisions` | **Which decisions were made, by whom, on what information** | Attach each decision to the information available then, not to the information available now. |
| 4 | `pressure` | **What pressure was acting on the decision** | Time, hierarchy, an audience, a family member, a radio that would not clear. Pressure is a cause, not an excuse, and it is usually designable. |
| 5 | `competencies` | **Which of the named competencies were exercised, and what was observed** | Tie the discussion back to the observable behaviours on the competency records so that the debrief produces something comparable across sessions. |
| 6 | `authority` | **Which real standard or protocol governs the part we are now arguing about** | The moment a debrief starts arguing about the correct procedure, it has left this pack's scope. Name the body, go and read the actual standard, and bring it back. |
| 7 | `change` | **What will be different, who owns it, by when** | A debrief with no owner and no date has produced agreement rather than change. |
| 8 | `people` | **Who needs something after this, and what is the route** | Held separately and last, and it is not a clinical intervention. The route belongs to the employer and to the behavioural health authority. |
